# Low-Dose Aripiprazole (LDA) for Long COVID — Curated Research Summary
*Compiled by Cleo — June 2026*

---

## Overview

Aripiprazole (brand name: Abilify) is an atypical antipsychotic approved for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. At standard doses (10–30 mg/day) it acts broadly on dopamine and serotonin receptors. At **very low doses (0.25–2 mg/day)** — 5–20x below the minimum psychiatric dose — it has a fundamentally different pharmacological profile that has generated significant interest in Long COVID and ME/CFS.

---

## Evidence Base

### 1. Retrospective Observational Study (Stanford LC Clinic, 2025)
**Citation:** Cui J et al. "Low-Dose Aripiprazole is a Promising Drug for Long COVID Treatment: A Retrospective Observational Study." *Open Forum Infectious Diseases.* 2026 Jan 11;13(Suppl 1):ofaf695.1788. PMCID: PMC12792691.
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12792691/
**Presented:** IDSA Annual Meeting, October 22, 2025

- **n = 50** adult LC patients from a dedicated LC clinic
- Treatment period: May 2021 – October 2023
- **Dose:** 0.1–2.0 mg/day via titration
- **Median symptom duration at treatment start:** 566.5 days (~19 months)
- **62% female**, median age 45, BMI 28.8

**Results:**
- Functional Status Scale (FSS) significantly improved (adjusted p=0.0149; effect size −0.580)
- Composite Symptom Score (CCS) significantly improved (p<0.001)
- **22% of patients** achieved ≥30% reduction in composite symptom scores (clinically meaningful threshold)
- 12% experienced worsening; 66% showed no significant change
- Modest improvements in: difficulty sleeping (effect size −0.390), fatigue (−0.363), headache (−0.321)
  - ⚠️ None of the symptom-level improvements survived multiple testing correction (Benjamini-Hochberg)
- Symptom duration >365 days did NOT affect treatment response — chronic patients responded as well as earlier-stage patients

**Conclusion:** LDA associated with functional improvement; RCT warranted. Neuroinflammatory mechanism exploration recommended.

**Note:** Senior author Linda Geng, MD PhD (Stanford) disclosed Pfizer grant/research support.

### 2. Large Patient-Reported Outcomes Survey (PNAS, 2025)
**Citation:** "Patient-reported treatment outcomes in ME/CFS and long COVID." *PNAS.* 2025 Jul 15;122(28):e2426874122. doi: 10.1073/pnas.2426874122
**URL:** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2426874122

- **n = 3,925** patients with ME/CFS or Long COVID
- Assessed experiences with **150+ treatments**
- Net Assessment Score (NAS) = % improved minus % worsened

**LDA-specific findings:**
- **LDA ≤2 mg:** NAS = +30% (−24% worsened)
- **Aripiprazole >2 mg:** NAS = −6% (−33% worsened) ⚠️

**Interpretation:** Dose matters enormously. At ≤2 mg, net benefit. Above 2 mg, net harm. This strongly reinforces the microdose-specific hypothesis.

**Top performers in the same survey (for context):**
- Pacing, fluids/electrolytes: highest NAS overall
- LDN (low-dose naltrexone): strong positive signal
- Antihistamines (H1+H2 combo): 63% NAS
- Mestinon (pyridostigmine): strong autonomic benefit
- ADHD stimulants: 42% NAS (−21% worsened)
- Ivabradine: 67% NAS (for POTS cluster)

**Poorly-rated comparators:**
- GET (graded exercise therapy): −72% NAS
- SSRIs (excl. fluvoxamine): 13% NAS (−27% worsened)
- Fluvoxamine: 6% NAS (−24% worsened) ⚠️ *See note below*
- CBT: 2% NAS (−24% worsened)

> ⚠️ **Important caveat on fluvoxamine:** The PNAS survey was conducted before the REVIVE-TOGETHER RCT published (Reis et al., 2026, AIM), which showed significant fatigue reduction. Patient-reported surveys have selection bias — patients who had bad experiences are more likely to report. The survey NAS for fluvoxamine does not supersede the RCT evidence.

### 3. Mechanistic Review — Buonsenso, 2026
**Citation:** Buonsenso D. "Abilify at a Whisper: Why Low-Dose Aripiprazole Is One of the Most Interesting Off-Label Options in Long COVID and ME/CFS." *Substack — Practical Pediatric Infectious Disease.* April 19, 2026.
**URL:** https://danilobuonsenso.substack.com/p/abilify-at-a-whisper-why-low-dose
**Note:** Expert opinion/synthesis piece by a credible LC researcher; not peer-reviewed.

**Key mechanistic claims:**
- At low doses, aripiprazole acts as a **partial dopamine agonist / D2 receptor stabilizer** — restores depleted dopaminergic tone rather than blocking it
- **Neuroinflammation evidence:** Neuroinflammation confirmed in ME/CFS via TSPO-PET imaging (Nakatomi et al., *J Nucl Med*, 2014) and in Long COVID via JAMA Psychiatry 2023 (Braga et al.)
- **Genomic overlap:** 82/377 genes altered by aripiprazole are also dysregulated in COVID-19; 93.9% show reversed expression pattern. p = 3.2×10⁻¹¹. Most enriched pathways: immune response and hyperinflammation.
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Reduces TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, fractalkine; inhibits NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome; suppresses microglial activation
- **H1 antihistamine activity:** Mild — relevant to MCAS component
- **Sigma-1 receptor agonism:** Same receptor targeted by fluvoxamine

**Source:** Medscape, "New Data: The Most Promising Treatments for Long COVID," November 2024
**URL:** https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/new-data-most-promising-treatments-long-covid-2024a1000lm5

Dr. Nisha Viswanathan, MD (Director, UCLA Long COVID Program):
> *"Abilify is traditionally used for the treatment of schizophrenia or other psychotic disorders, but in a low-dose format, there is some data to suggest that it can also be anti-inflammatory and helpful for cognitive issues like brain fog."*

## Proposed Mechanisms at Low Dose (0.25–2 mg)

| Mechanism | Relevance to Long COVID |
|---|---|
| Partial D2 agonism / dopamine restoration | Depleted dopaminergic tone → fatigue, anhedonia, brain fog |
| Microglial suppression | Neuroinflammation confirmed in LC/ME-CFS by PET imaging |
| TNF-α / IL-6 / IL-1β / NLRP3 reduction | Cytokine dysregulation is a core LC finding |
| NF-κB inhibition | Viral persistence-driven inflammatory signaling |
| Mild H1 antihistamine | MCAS component common in LC |
| Sigma-1 receptor agonism | Shared mechanism with fluvoxamine; ER stress reduction |
| HPA axis modulation | Dopamine directly modulates HPA; relevant to cortisol dysregulation |

## Dosing

- **Starting dose:** 0.25 mg/day (commonly reported in LC clinic practice)
- **Titration range:** 0.25 → 0.5 → 1.0 → 2.0 mg/day
- **Maximum for LC use:** 2 mg/day — patient-reported data shows harm above this threshold
- **Standard psychiatric dose:** 10–30 mg/day (entirely different pharmacological profile)
- **Formulation note:** Standard tablets start at 2 mg; LC clinicians typically use compounded 0.25 mg or liquid formulation for microdosing

---

## Side Effects / Safety Considerations

- Generally well-tolerated at low doses
- Akathisia (inner restlessness) — rare at ≤2 mg but possible
- Sedation — possible, especially early; can be used to advantage if sleep is a target
- Weight gain — minimal at low doses compared to standard doses
- **No significant CYP interactions at low doses** unlike fluvoxamine (which is a strong CYP1A2/2D6 inhibitor)
- Activation effects: some patients report increased energy (positive); others report worsening anxiety
- PNAS survey: 24% reported worsening at ≤2 mg — not trivial, needs monitoring

---

## Evidence Gaps / Limitations

- No RCT published yet — the Stanford study explicitly calls for one
- PNAS survey is self-reported, not randomized; subject to selection bias
- 22% response rate in the Stanford study — majority did not show clinically meaningful improvement
- Mechanism is largely inferred from preclinical data and genomic analysis
- No long-term safety data in this specific population at these doses
- Unclear which LC phenotypes are most likely to respond

## Positioning Relative to Other LC Treatments

| Drug | Evidence Level | Best for | Phenotype fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LDA ≤2 mg** | Observational (n=50) + patient survey | Brain fog, fatigue, MCAS, dysautonomia | Neuro/autonomic dominant |
| **Fluvoxamine** | RCT (n=399, REVIVE-TOGETHER 2026) | Fatigue, QOL | Gut viral persistence, inflammation-driven |
| **LDN** | Multiple observational + small RCTs | Fatigue, pain, sleep, brain fog, PEM | Broad; immune/neuroimmune |
| **Valacyclovir** | Mechanistic + specialist use | EBV/HSV reactivation | Viral persistence subtype |
| **Mestinon** | Small studies + strong patient data | POTS, dysautonomia | Autonomic dominant |
| **Ivabradine** | Small studies + patient data (67% NAS) | POTS | Autonomic dominant |
