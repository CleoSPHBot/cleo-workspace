## New Trial Results

### 1. RECOVER-VITAL: Long-Duration Paxlovid Fails for Long COVID (March 2026)
**Status:** Published results — CONFIRMED NULL

The NIH RECOVER initiative's RECOVER-VITAL trial (results posted late March 2026) tested 15-day and 25-day courses of nirmatrelvir–ritonavir (Paxlovid) vs. standard 5-day control in adults with established Long COVID. Primary outcomes included post-exertional malaise, cognitive dysfunction, and orthostatic intolerance.

**Result:** None of the extended Paxlovid arms showed significant improvement vs. control on any primary outcome.

**Significance:** This effectively closes the door on viral persistence as the primary operative mechanism for *established* Long COVID amenable to currently available antivirals — at least at these durations and in unselected patients.

### 2. RECOVER ENERGIZE: Metformin Fails as Treatment for Established Long COVID (March 2026)
**Status:** Published results — CONFIRMED NULL

The RECOVER ENERGIZE arm tested metformin 1,000 mg/day for 6 months in adults with established Long COVID. Result: no improvement vs. placebo.

**Critical distinction to preserve:** Metformin *prevention* evidence remains strong (COVID-OUT: 41% LC risk reduction; ACTIV-6: 50% risk reduction) when started within 3–7 days of acute infection. Treatment ≠ prevention.

### 4. Monocyte Oxidative Stress in Long-COVID POTS (medRxiv Preprint, May 2026)
**Status:** New preprint — NOT indexed

Vanderbilt University Medical Center team (Abd-Eldayem et al.) posted a medRxiv preprint (May 8, 2026) demonstrating that monocyte oxidative stress underlies persistent immune activation specifically in Long COVID patients with postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS). This adds mechanistic evidence for the dysautonomia-inflammation link.

**Significance:** Provides a cellular mechanism (monocyte ROS production) for persistent immune activation in LC-POTS, a subpopulation-specific finding with potential therapeutic implications (antioxidant targeting).

### 5. Lumbrokinase for Long COVID, Lyme, and ME/CFS (NCT06511050)
**Status:** Recruiting — Phase 1/2, Mount Sinai

The Cohen Center for Recovery from Complex Chronic Illnesses (CoRE) at Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai is running a Phase 1/2 trial of lumbrokinase (an earthworm-derived fibrinolytic enzyme) in adults with Long COVID, Post-treatment Lyme Disease Syndrome (PTLDS), and ME/CFS. n=120. Primary completion November 2026.

**Rationale:** Lumbrokinase targets microclot/fibrin pathology, which has been proposed as a key driver of Long COVID symptoms (elevated fibrinogen, microclot formation from spike protein interaction).

## Subgroup & Phenotype Insights

### 7. PYCNOVID: Functional Capacity Assessment in PCC (PLoS ONE, 2026)
**Status:** New peer-reviewed paper — NOT indexed

Braun J, Kopp J, Künzi L, Puhan MA, Fehr JS, Radtke T (2026). From the PYCNOVID randomized controlled trial (n=150 adults with PCC, 74.7% female). The study validated the 30-second sit-to-stand test (30s-STS) as a feasible, safe functional capacity measure in post-COVID condition.

**Key findings:**
- 46% of participants had z-scores below the 5th percentile (severely impaired functional capacity)
- Very severe PEM subgroup performed worst on 30s-STS
- Confirmed large phenotypic heterogeneity — PEM grade correlates with functional impairment
- Test was safe (no adverse events), practical for clinical monitoring

**Significance:** Provides a low-cost, validated clinical tool for subgroup stratification by PEM severity — important for future trial enrollment and outcome tracking.

**Reference:**
- Braun J, Kopp J, Künzi L, Puhan MA, Fehr JS, Radtke T.
- *Measurement properties of the 30-second sit-to-stand test in post COVID-19 condition: Results from the PYCNOVID randomised controlled trial.*
- PLoS ONE, 2026
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0348275
- URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0348275&type=printable

## Mechanism & Biomarker Discoveries

### 9. LC-Mo: Distinct CD14+ Monocyte State Linked to Long COVID Fatigue (Nature Immunology, 2026)
**Status:** Peer-reviewed, published — NOT indexed

Kumar S, Li C, Zhou L, Zhan Q, Alaswad A, Volland S, Costa B, Krooss SA, et al. (MHH Hannover / Radboud / Yale). Single-cell analysis of immune cells from Long COVID patients identified a distinct molecular state in CD14+ monocytes ("LC-Mo") particularly prevalent in patients who had mild-to-moderate acute COVID-19.

**Key findings:**
- LC-Mo was elevated in LC patients vs. recovered controls
- LC-Mo levels correlated with severity of fatigue and respiratory symptoms
- LC-Mo associated with elevated plasma cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α)
- Approach: classified patients by severity of *original* infection, enabling cleaner signal extraction
- Potential future utility: as a biomarker for Long COVID subtyping and precision medicine targets

**Significance:** One of the strongest mechanistic biomarker findings of 2026 — links a specific immune cell state to Long COVID symptom severity, providing a molecular target.

**Reference:**
- Saumya Kumar, Chaofan Li, Liang Zhou, Qiuyao Zhan, Ahmed Alaswad, Sonja Volland, Bibiana Costa, Simon Alexander Krooss, Isabel Klefenz, Hagen Schmaus, Antonia Zeuzem, Dorothee von Witzendorff, Helena Lickei, Lea Pueschel, Anke R.M.
