Techniques and procedures used in the MGH Autonomic Lab for 
Autonomic Reflex Screen Testing 
CNAP® (Continuous Noninvasive Arterial Pressure) with double 
finger sensor technology, is used for recording continuous 
(beat-to-beat) blood pressure. 3-Lead continuous ECG monitoring 
is used throughout the testing. 
Q-Sweat test is performed for evaluation of the integrity of the 
postganglionic sympathetic sudomotor fibers (primarily 
cholinergic), with the use of 10% acetylcholine solution 
iontophoresis technique to measure indirect reflex sweat response 
through changes in humidity, recorded from 4 standard locations 
of ventral forearm, upper lateral shin, lower medial shin and 
dorsal foot.
70° Head-Up-Tilt is performed after 10 minutes of resting quietly 
in supine position with stable baseline BP and HR. Then the 
patient is tilted-up to 70°, and is returned to supine after 10 
min or until patient experiences problematic severe orthostatic 
intolerance, presyncopal symptoms, or syncope, in which case the 
test is terminated prematurely.
Valsalva Maneuver is performed in supine position with expiratory 
pressure to be maintained ideally at 40 mmHg for 15 seconds by 
blowing through a disposable COVID-filtered mouthpiece attached 
to a digital manometer built in WR medical autonomic testing 
equipment. If this pressure cannot be maintained, lesser 
pressures are acceptable only if they are sufficient to elicit 
appropriate physiologic responses. The test is done at least 
twice with 2 min rest period in between, to analyze blood 
pressure changes and pulse valsalva ratio of the best recording. 
In the case of a table top/flat top/square wave response or other 
technical challenges, this maneuver may be repeated again sitting 
up.
Heart rate variability with deep breathing is performed in supine 
position, after a 5 min period of resting, with 5 seconds 
inspiration and 5 seconds expiration cycles for 1 minute (6 
breaths per minute) using timed breathing cues. The test is done 
twice with 2 min rest period in between, to analyze the best 
heart rate changes recorded for a 5 consecutive breathing cycles.

Q-Sweat test is performed for evaluation of the integrity of the postganglionic sympathetic sudomotor fibers (primarily 
cholinergic), with the use of 10% acetylcholine solution iontophoresis technique to measure indirect reflex sweat 
response through changes in humidity, recorded from 4 standard locations of ventral forearm, upper lateral shin, lower 
medial shin and dorsal foot.
70° Head-Up-Tilt is performed after 10 minutes of resting quietly in supine position with stable baseline BP and HR. 
Then the patient is tilted-up to 70°, and is returned to supine after 10 min or until patient experiences problematic 
severe orthostatic intolerance, presyncopal symptoms, or syncope, in which case the test is terminated prematurely.
Valsalva Maneuver is performed in supine position with expiratory pressure to be maintained ideally at 40 mmHg for
