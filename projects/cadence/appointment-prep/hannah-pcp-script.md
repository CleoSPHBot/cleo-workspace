# Script for a Skeptical PCP
*For when a doctor isn't immediately receptive to your Long COVID diagnosis*
*Read through before your appointment. Use the phrases that feel natural.*

---

## The Core Challenge

Some PCPs still think Long COVID is anxiety, deconditioning, or "just fatigue." They may:
- Say your labs are normal (they often are in LC)
- Suggest graded exercise therapy (this can make you worse)
- Attribute everything to depression or anxiety
- Be dismissive of symptoms they can't immediately explain

**You are not making this up. Your labs, your biometrics, and peer-reviewed science back you.**

---

## Your Opening

Start neutral and professional. Don't lead with frustration, even if you feel it.

*"I've been dealing with symptoms since my COVID infection in [year] that I believe are consistent with Long COVID. I've done quite a bit of research and I have objective data I'd like to share with you. I'm hoping we can work together on a treatment plan."*

---

## Your Objective Data — Lead With This

Don't start with how you feel. Start with numbers. Doctors respond to data.

**Lab findings:**
- *"My EBV Early Antigen IgM was reactive in June 2025 — that indicates active EBV reactivation, which has been documented in approximately 60–70% of Long COVID patients in published research."*
- *"My ESR was 30 in June, dropped to 10 in July, and was 20 in September — oscillating inflammation over three months."*
- *"I have a positive anti-cardiolipin IgM — an autoimmune marker consistent with the hypercoagulation documented in Long COVID."*
- *"My colonoscopy in September 2025 showed patchy healing ulcers and stool occult blood was positive — gut wall involvement."*
- *"My LH, FSH, and estradiol have been suppressed — consistent with hypothalamic-pituitary dysregulation from Long COVID."*

**Biometrics:**
- *"I wear a WHOOP device continuously. My average HRV over the past 90 days is 32ms — typical healthy adults my age are 50–100ms. My SpO2 averages 93.4%, which is below the 95% clinical threshold."*
- *"I have clear post-exertional malaise with a 3-day lag — I can show you the biometric data where exertion on one day corresponds with recovery drops 3 days later."*

---

## If They Say "Your Labs Are Normal"

*"I understand many standard labs are normal in Long COVID — that's actually well-documented in the literature. The 2021 ME/CFS Clinician Coalition guidelines specifically note that normal CBC and metabolic panels don't rule out ME/CFS or Long COVID. The abnormalities I have — EBV reactivation, anti-cardiolipin IgM, oscillating ESR, suppressed reproductive hormones — are consistent with published Long COVID phenotypes."*

---

## If They Suggest It's Anxiety or Depression

Don't get defensive. Be factual.

*"I understand why you might consider that — fatigue and brain fog can look like depression. But I want to flag a few things. First, my symptoms started immediately after a documented COVID infection, not before it. Second, my HRV data shows objective physiological dysfunction — that's not anxiety. Third, a 2025 JAMA Psychiatry meta-analysis of over 100,000 patients found GLP-1 treatment — which works on neuroinflammation — improved cognitive function in Long COVID, which wouldn't be expected if this were purely psychiatric."*

*"I'm open to treating any anxiety that's developed as a result of being chronically ill. But I'd like to address the underlying biology too."*

---

## If They Suggest Graded Exercise Therapy (GET)

This one is important. GET can cause serious harm in Long COVID with PEM.

*"I've read about that approach, and I want to flag something — the WHO, CDC, and NICE guidelines have all updated their guidance to caution against graded exercise therapy in patients with post-exertional malaise, which I clearly have. A 2022 review found 81% of ME/CFS patients reported harm from GET. The recommended approach for patients with PEM is pacing, not progressive exercise. I'd feel more comfortable with a pacing-based approach."*

---

## If They're Unfamiliar with Long COVID Treatments

*"I've been reviewing the PLRC and RTHM Long COVID Treatment Guide, published March 2026 — it's a collaboration between the Patient-Led Research Collaborative and a Long COVID telehealth clinic. It covers about 20 treatments with response rate data from a Harvard/Stanford study of 3,925 patients. I'd love to go through some of the options that match my phenotype."*

---

## Your Specific Asks (pick the ones most relevant)

**Valacyclovir** (most important — you have the EBV evidence)
*"My EBV Early Antigen IgM was reactive. The Iwasaki Lab at Yale has documented EBV reactivation as a driver of Long COVID, and the standard antiviral approach is valacyclovir 1g three times daily. The ME/CFS Clinician Coalition guidelines list antivirals for confirmed viral reactivation. Would you be willing to prescribe this?"*

**LDN (Low-Dose Naltrexone)** (easiest ask — very safe, cheap, widely used)
*"Low-dose naltrexone at 1.5–4.5mg has been shown in multiple cohort studies to improve fatigue and quality of life in 34–58% of Long COVID patients. It's inexpensive, has a very favorable safety profile, and is recommended in the Mayo Clinic ME/CFS guidelines. Would you be open to trying this?"*

**Referral to a Long COVID specialist**
*"If you're not comfortable managing this yourself, I completely understand — Long COVID is complex. I'd like a referral to a Long COVID specialist or clinic. Boston Medical Center has a ReCOVer Long COVID Clinic that's part of the NIH RECOVER research network."*

**Antihistamines (H1+H2)**
*"A combination of an H1 antihistamine like cetirizine and an H2 antihistamine like famotidine has shown benefit in Long COVID patients with mast cell activation features, which I believe I have. These are over-the-counter, but I wanted to flag that I'm considering adding them."*

---

## If the Appointment Goes Badly

It's okay to end gracefully and try elsewhere.

*"Thank you for your time. I hear that you have a different perspective on this. I'm going to continue working with my other providers and may seek a second opinion. I do want to make sure you're aware that I'm experiencing significant functional impairment — I'm currently not able to work/study [as applicable] because of this."*

**Document everything:** After the appointment, send a follow-up message through the patient portal summarizing what was discussed. This creates a paper trail.

---

## Your Rights

- You can ask for a second opinion. Always.
- You can request that your symptoms be documented in your chart, even if the doctor doesn't know how to treat them.
- You can ask for a referral even if the doctor is skeptical.
- Cleo can help you draft a follow-up letter or portal message after the appointment.

## Remember

The data is on your side. You have:
- Reactive EBV serology

- Oscillating ESR over 3 draws

- Colonoscopy findings
- 90 days of objective biometric dysfunction

That is not anxiety. That is biology.
